Patients message clinics at all hours, and many of those messages are the same few questions. An AI assistant can answer them instantly. But a dental clinic is not an online shop, and some messages need a person. The trick is drawing a clear line.

## What AI handles well

These are routine, factual and low-risk:

- opening hours, address, parking and directions
- which treatments the clinic offers
- general price ranges the clinic has published
- booking, rescheduling and cancelling appointments
- sending forms and pre-visit instructions the clinic has written

For all of these, the AI answers from information the clinic provides and approves, not from the open internet.

## What AI must never handle

Some messages always go to a person, no matter how confident the AI sounds:

- **Anything clinical.** Pain, swelling, bleeding, medication questions, "is this normal?" These are flagged to staff straight away, and urgent ones are marked as urgent.
- **Payments and refunds.** Money questions go to the practice team.
- **Complaints.** An unhappy patient deserves a human reply.

The AI's job in these cases is simply to say a team member will reply, and to make sure that happens.

## Keep people in control

A few rules make the difference between helpful and risky:

- The AI works from an approved list of answers and can say "I'll pass this to the team" whenever it isn't sure.
- Staff can see every conversation and step in at any time.
- New or unusual replies can be set to wait for staff approval before they are sent.
- Patients always know they can ask for a person.

## Start small

Begin with booking and the most common questions only. Read the conversations for a few weeks, adjust the answers, and widen the scope once your team is comfortable.
