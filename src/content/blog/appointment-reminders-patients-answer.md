Most clinics already send some kind of reminder. The problem is that many of them are one-way: a message goes out, nobody replies, and reception still phones everyone the day before to be sure. A good reminder workflow does three jobs at once. It reminds, it collects an answer, and it does something useful with that answer.

## Send two reminders, not one

A single reminder the evening before leaves no time to refill the slot if the patient cancels. Two messages work better:

- **48 hours before:** gives the patient time to reschedule and gives you time to offer the slot to someone else.
- **24 hours before (or the morning of):** a short nudge for patients who confirmed but might still forget.

Skip the second message for patients who already confirmed by replying to the first, so nobody feels chased.

## Ask for one tap, not a phone call

The easiest reply wins. Give patients three clear options in the message itself:

1. Confirm
2. Reschedule
3. Cancel

On WhatsApp these can be buttons. By SMS, a simple "reply 1, 2 or 3" works. What matters is that the patient never has to call the clinic to answer.

## Write it like a person from the clinic

Keep it short and specific: the patient's first name, the day and time, the clinician, and the clinic name. Leave out marketing. A message that reads like a note from reception gets answered; a message that reads like a campaign gets ignored.

## Do something with every answer

This is where most setups stop too early. Each reply should update the appointment automatically:

- **Confirmed:** mark it in the schedule. No call needed.
- **Reschedule:** reply with a few open slots the patient can pick from.
- **Cancel:** release the slot and offer it to patients on the waitlist, in order, until someone takes it.

## Keep a person in the loop

Automation should take the routine work away, not hide problems. Two simple rules help:

- Patients who don't answer either message go on a short list for reception to call personally.
- Every morning, reception gets one summary: who confirmed, who hasn't answered, and which gaps are still open.

## Respect preferences

Use the channel each patient prefers, honour opt-outs straight away, and never put clinical details in a reminder. The message only needs the time, the place and the options.
