Every practice has a recall list, and in most practices it keeps growing. Patients mean to book their check-up, life gets in the way, and the clinic never quite has the time to chase them. A recall workflow turns that list into a small, steady routine.

## Find the right patients every week

Once a week, the workflow pulls patients from the practice software who are:

- due for a check-up or cleaning in the next few weeks, or
- overdue by a month or more.

Running it weekly keeps each batch small, so the clinic never gets a sudden wave of bookings it can't fit in.

## Make the message personal

Generic recall letters are easy to ignore. A short message works better when it includes:

- the patient's first name
- what they are due for, in plain words
- family members due at the same time, so a parent can book everyone at once
- a direct link to open slots

AI can help draft these messages from a template the practice approves, so they read naturally without anyone writing each one.

## Let patients book from the message

The most important part: the patient should be able to book without calling. A link to available slots, or a reply in WhatsApp that offers a few times, removes the step where most people give up.

## Follow up once, then stop

If there's no reply after a week, send one gentle follow-up. After that, stop. Patients who still haven't answered can go on a short list for a personal call, or simply wait for the next recall cycle. Nobody should feel chased.

## Respect choices

Use each patient's preferred channel, honour opt-outs immediately, and keep clinical details out of the message. The goal is a helpful nudge, not marketing.
